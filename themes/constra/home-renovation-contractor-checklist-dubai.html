Home Renovation Contractors in Dubai: Before You Hire

  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. Contractor Checklist

If you are comparing home renovation contractors in Dubai right now, you are probably doing what most owners do: collecting a few quotes, picking the one that feels most trustworthy or comes in cheapest, and getting ready to sign. That approach works fine when nothing goes wrong. The problem is that on a villa-scale renovation, something almost always does.

None of this means you need to abandon the contractor you like. It means there is a short list of checks worth doing before the contract is signed, not after the first invoice arrives higher than expected. This page walks through exactly what those checks are.

What to Check Before You Sign With a Renovation Contractor

  • The quote breakdown, not just the total. A single lump-sum figure hides where the money actually goes. Ask for a line-item breakdown so you can see material, labour, and margin separately, and compare it against other quotes on equal terms.
  • What counts as a variation. Almost every renovation has changes along the way. The contract should define, in writing, how a variation gets priced and approved before the work happens, not after.
  • Payment stages tied to actual progress. Payments should follow verified completion of defined stages, not the calendar. A payment schedule based purely on dates gives the contractor no reason to keep pace.
  • Insurance and liability cover. Confirm the contractor holds valid liability insurance and workers' compensation cover for the full duration of the project, not just at the point the quote was issued.
  • References from a project of similar scale. A contractor who does excellent kitchen fit-outs is not automatically equipped for a full villa renovation. Ask for references from a project close to your scope and speak to the owner directly.
  • Who inspects the work. If the answer is nobody independent, every quality decision on site is effectively being made by the same party being paid to finish quickly.
  • The defects liability period. Confirm in writing how long the contractor remains responsible for fixing issues that appear after handover, and what happens if they do not respond.

Contractor Alone vs. Contractor With Independent Oversight

This is not a case against hiring a contractor directly. You need one either way. It is a look at what changes when someone independent is reviewing the numbers and the work alongside them.

  Contractor Only Contractor + Owner's Representative
Quote review Accepted largely on trust   Line-item reviewed against market rates before signing
Variation orders Priced and approved by the contractor   Independently assessed before approval
Payment certification Paid against invoices submitted   Paid against verified, inspected progress
Site quality checks   Self-reported by the contractor   Regular independent inspections
Who you call with a problem The party responsible for the problem   A representative who reports only to you
