Before You Sign With a Contractor: 12 Things You Must Check in Dubai

You've probably compared prices, reviewed designs, and shortlisted a contractor for your project. But most issues in Dubai projects don't come from design. They come from what wasn't checked before signing. In this guide, you'll see exactly what to verify so you can avoid delays, disputes, and costly mistakes.

KEY TAKEAWAYS:
  • Contractor due diligence is the process of verifying a contractor's legal status, experience, and capability before signing an agreement.
  • Licensed contractors in Dubai must be registered with relevant authorities and operate within approved classifications.
  • Clear contracts with defined scope, payment terms, and timelines reduce disputes and cost overruns significantly.
  • Verification of insurance and approvals protects clients from financial and legal liability during construction.
  • Project success depends on contractor capability, not just price competitiveness.
  • Early-stage checks prevent delays, rework, and quality failures during construction.
  • Professional oversight from a project manager improves transparency and reduces execution risk before and during the project.

What Is Contractor Due Diligence in Dubai?

Contractor due diligence is the process of verifying a contractor's legal status, experience, and capability before signing an agreement. In the Dubai context, this means checking licences, registration status, insurance coverage, past project performance, and financial standing before any commitment is made.

Due diligence is not bureaucratic box-ticking. It is the single most effective way to reduce the risk of project failure before a single dirham changes hands. In Dubai, the regulatory landscape for contractors is specific and enforced, which means hiring an unlicensed or improperly classified contractor exposes you to legal liability, not just poor workmanship.

For homeowners and investors considering villa renovation project management in Dubai, the due diligence process should begin well before a contractor is shortlisted, and certainly long before a contract is signed.

Why Contractor Checks Matter Before Signing in Dubai

Poor contractor selection is the leading cause of construction disputes, cost overruns, and project delays in the UAE residential sector. The financial and legal consequences of signing with the wrong contractor can be severe and long-lasting.

Construction disputes in the Middle East remain a persistent problem. A 2023 report by Arcadis found that the average construction dispute in the region took 15.4 months to resolve — Source: Arcadis Global Construction Disputes Report, 2023. For a villa renovation project, a dispute of that duration means living with an incomplete home for over a year while legal costs accumulate.

Dubai Law No. 7 of 2025 requires all contractors to be registered in a unified contractor registry. Hiring a contractor who is not registered under this framework exposes you to permit refusals, stop-work orders, and potential liability for any resulting damages. For construction risk management in Dubai, this regulatory shift makes pre-signing verification more important than ever.

The 12 Critical Checks Before Signing With a Contractor in Dubai

The 12 checks every client should complete before signing a contractor agreement in Dubai cover licensing, experience, contractual terms, insurance, approvals, and oversight structures. Working through all 12 takes time. Skipping them costs significantly more.

Check 1: Verify the Contractor's Licence

First, every contractor operating in Dubai must hold a valid licence issued by the Department of Economy and Tourism (DET, formerly DED) and, where applicable, a classification certificate from Dubai Municipality. You can verify both through the official Dubai REST portal or directly through the Dubai Municipality contractor portal.

A valid licence confirms the contractor is legally permitted to trade. The classification certificate confirms they are approved to carry out the specific type and scale of work your project requires. A contractor licensed for fit-out work, for example, is not automatically approved to carry out structural works on a villa.

Check 2: Confirm Registration Under Dubai Law No. 7 of 2025

In Dubai, contractors must be licensed, registered, and classified according to the type and scale of work they perform. Ask your contractor directly for their registry number and verify it through the relevant authority portal before any contract discussions proceed.

This is a relatively recent requirement, and many clients are unaware of it. Contractors who cannot produce their registry number should be immediately removed from your shortlist.

Check 3: Review Relevant Project Experience

Third, relevant experience is not the same as general experience. A contractor with 20 years of commercial fit-out experience is not automatically qualified to build or renovate a luxury villa in Emirates Hills. Ask for a portfolio of directly comparable projects completed in the UAE, with client references you can actually contact.

For villa renovation project management in Dubai, verifying that your contractor has completed at least three to five projects of similar type, value, and complexity in the past five years is a minimum standard. Ask for final account figures, not just original contract values.

Check 4: Assess Scope of Work Clarity

Fourth, a contractor who cannot produce a detailed, itemised scope of work before signing is a contractor who will recover their margin through variations after signing. The scope document should define every element of the work, including materials specifications, finishes, equipment brands, and exclusions.

Vague scope documents are one of the most common sources of construction disputes in Dubai. If the contract says "supply and install kitchen" without specifying the brand, grade, and configuration of every element, you have no contractual basis to challenge what the contractor eventually provides.

Check 5: Scrutinise Contract Terms and Clauses

A well-structured construction contract defines scope, cost, timeline, and liability to reduce project risk. Before signing, the contract must clearly address: the mechanism for issuing and pricing variations, the process for resolving disputes, the conditions under which either party may terminate, the liquidated damages applicable for delays, and the defects liability period following practical completion.

For project management services in Dubai, one of the most valuable early interventions is a contract review by an experienced professional before it is signed. Many standard contractor-issued contracts heavily favour the contractor. Clients who sign them without review often discover this only when something goes wrong.

Check 6: Evaluate Payment Structure and Milestones

Sixth, payment terms should always be linked to verified construction milestones, never to calendar dates or contractor invoices alone. A common and costly mistake is agreeing to front-heavy payment schedules that give the contractor significant funds before significant work has been completed.

A reasonable payment structure for a villa renovation in Dubai typically looks like this: 10% mobilisation advance, with the balance paid in progress-linked tranches certified by an independent project manager or consultant. For cost control in construction projects, the payment structure negotiated before signing is often the most important single financial decision of the entire project.

Check 7: Verify Insurance and Liability Coverage

Seventh, contractors working on UAE villa projects must carry adequate insurance. At minimum, this should include contractors all-risk (CAR) insurance covering the full reinstatement value of the works, third-party public liability insurance, and employer's liability insurance for all workers on site.

Ask for certificates of insurance directly from the contractor's insurer, not a copy produced by the contractor. Verify the coverage amounts, the named insured, and the policy expiry dates. Verifying a contractor licence in the UAE involves checking DET registration, municipality approvals, and insurance coverage, and all three must be current at the point of signing.

Check 8: Confirm Approvals and Authority Co-ordination

Eighth, most villa renovation and construction works in Dubai require permits and approvals from Dubai Municipality, the relevant master developer (Emaar, Nakheel, DGDA, etc.), and in some cases DEWA and Etisalat. Confirm which approvals are required for your specific project and who is responsible for obtaining them.

A common mistake is assuming the contractor will handle all approvals as part of their scope. This assumption should be written into the contract explicitly, not left as an implied obligation. Your project manager should map out every required approval and the associated lead times before the construction programme is agreed.

Check 9: Test Programme and Timeline Realism

Ninth, a contractor who quotes an unrealistically short programme is either underestimating the complexity of the work or planning to run multiple projects simultaneously with insufficient resources. Both outcomes result in delays to your project.

Ask the contractor to provide a detailed programme showing the sequence of activities, the resources allocated to each, and the critical path through the work. An experienced project manager can review this programme and identify whether it is realistic before you commit to it as a contractual obligation.

Check 10: Understand Subcontractor Management

Tenth, on most villa projects the main contractor will subcontract significant portions of the work, including MEP, tiling, joinery, and landscaping. You have a right to know who these subcontractors are, and you should satisfy yourself that they are appropriately qualified.

Ask for the contractor's list of approved subcontractors and their respective licences. The MEP subcontractor's credentials are particularly important, given the critical role of mechanical, electrical, and plumbing systems in UAE villa performance. A strong main contractor with a weak MEP subcontractor is still a significant risk to your project.

Check 11: Confirm Quality Standards and Specifications

Eleventh, quality standards should be defined in the contract by reference to specific standards: UAE construction standards, ASTM, BS, or EN specifications, or project-specific quality plans prepared by the design consultant. "Good workmanship" as a quality benchmark is meaningless in a contractual dispute.

For quality monitoring in villa construction, the contract should also specify the inspection and test plan that will govern quality verification throughout the project, who is responsible for carrying out inspections, and how defects will be identified, documented, and remedied.

Check 12: Establish Communication and Reporting Structure

Twelfth, and often overlooked, the contract should define how communication will be managed throughout the project. This includes the frequency and format of progress reports, the process for issuing and responding to instructions, the named representatives of each party, and the escalation path when disagreements arise.

A contractor who is resistant to formal reporting structures before signing will not become more transparent after signing. Clear communication and reporting obligations, written into the contract, are one of the most effective tools available for maintaining contractor accountability throughout construction.

What Are the Legal Requirements for Contractors in the UAE?

In Dubai, contractors must be licensed, registered, and classified according to the type and scale of work they perform. This three-tier requirement means that verifying a single licence is not sufficient. You need to check that the licence, classification, and registry status are all current, consistent, and appropriate for your project.

Dubai Municipality classifies contractors across multiple grades based on financial capacity, technical resources, and track record. A Grade 5 contractor, for example, can only carry out works up to a defined contract value threshold. Appointing a contractor outside their approved grade is a regulatory breach that can result in permit refusal and stop-work orders.

The introduction of Dubai Law No. 7 of 2025 adds a further layer of mandatory registration. This law creates a unified contractor registry that consolidates licensing, classification, and compliance data in one place. All new construction contracts in Dubai must reference a contractor with a valid registry number under this framework.

How to Verify a Contractor Licence in Dubai

Verifying a contractor licence in the UAE involves checking DET registration, municipality approvals, and insurance coverage, and all three can be confirmed through official government portals. Here is how to carry out each check:

  • DET licence verification: Visit the official Dubai Economy portal and use the business licence verification tool. Enter the contractor's trade licence number to confirm validity, expiry date, and permitted activities.
  • Dubai Municipality classification: Visit the Dubai Municipality portal and search the approved contractors list. Confirm the contractor's classification grade and ensure it is appropriate for your project type and contract value.
  • Unified registry verification (Law No. 7 of 2025): Use the relevant authority portal to verify the contractor's registry number. This step is now mandatory before any permit application can be submitted in Dubai.
  • Insurance certificate verification: Request the original certificate of insurance directly from the contractor's insurer, not a copy produced by the contractor. Confirm the coverage amounts and expiry dates independently.

What to Do Before You Sign: A Step-by-Step Process

Before signing any contractor agreement in Dubai, work through these steps in sequence:

  1. Appoint a project manager or consultant to run the contractor selection process independently.
  2. Prepare a detailed scope of work and draw up a list of prequalification requirements before issuing any invitation to tender.
  3. Issue tender documents to a minimum of three prequalified contractors and hold clarification meetings with each.
  4. Review all tender returns against the same evaluation criteria: price, programme, methodology, resources, and references.
  5. Verify the licence, classification, registry number, and insurance of your preferred contractor through official portals before entering contract negotiations.
  6. Negotiate contract terms with legal or professional support. Do not sign the contractor's standard form without independent review.
  7. Confirm that all required authority approvals are mapped, that responsibility for each is assigned in the contract, and that the programme accounts for approval lead times.
  8. Establish the payment schedule, linked to verified milestones, before signing.

Contractor Selection Is a Risk Decision, Not Just a Commercial One

Signing with a contractor in Dubai is not a commercial formality. It is a risk management decision that shapes every aspect of your project from that point forward.

The 12 checks in this guide cover the areas where most projects in the UAE fail. A contractor who passes all 12 is not a guarantee of success. But a contractor who fails any of them is a genuine risk to your budget, your programme, and your property.

If you are approaching the point of signing and have not yet completed these checks, pause. The time spent verifying now is a fraction of the time and cost that disputes, delays, and rework will take later.
